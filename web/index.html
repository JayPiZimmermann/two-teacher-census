What the explorer shows

A planar teacher network with two ReLU neurons, a student network with two, and the Gaussian population loss between them. Every row of the table above is a family of critical points of that loss, with its exact type — global minimum, spurious local minimum, or saddle — read off a theorem, not a numerical Hessian. The map colours each teacher by its census: which minima it has. Below are the statements; the proofs are Lean code.

Setting

Directions on the unit circle are written as angles. The teacher has directions \(\beta_0,\beta_1\) and signed masses \(s_0,s_1\); the student has directions \(\theta_0,\theta_1\) and signed masses \(c_0,c_1\). Two activations are compared, the plain ReLU \(\sigma_R(z)=\max(z,0)\) and the centered feature \(\sigma_C(z)=\tfrac12\lvert z\rvert\), the latter being what a learned linear skip connection turns the ReLU into (see the paper). For \(q\in\{C,R\}\) the population loss over a standard Gaussian input is a kernel sum, \[ 2\pi\,\mathscr L_q(c,\theta)=\tfrac{\kappa_q}{2}\bigl(c_0^2+c_1^2\bigr)+c_0c_1\,\Phi_q(\theta_0-\theta_1)-c_0P_q(\theta_0)-c_1P_q(\theta_1)+\tfrac{E_q}{2}, \] with the angular kernels \(\Phi_C(t)=(\tfrac\pi2-t)\cos t+\sin t\) and \(\Phi_R(t)=(\pi-t)\cos t+\sin t\) on \(0\le t\le\pi\), extended evenly with periods \(\pi\) and \(2\pi\); the diagonal values \(\kappa_C=\pi/2\), \(\kappa_R=\pi\); the teacher potential \(P_q(t)=s_0\Phi_q(t-\beta_0)+s_1\Phi_q(t-\beta_1)\) and its angular derivative \(A_q=-P_q'\); and a constant teacher self-energy \(E_q\). Write \(W(t)=s_0\lvert\sin(t-\beta_0)\rvert+s_1\lvert\sin(t-\beta_1)\rvert\) and \(\tau_q=P_q-2W\). Both losses are \(C^2\), and \[ \mathscr L_R=\mathscr L_C+\tfrac18\lVert u-v\rVert^2,\qquad u=\sum_i c_i(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i),\quad v=\sum_j s_j(\cos\beta_j,\sin\beta_j): \] the plain loss is the centered loss plus a penalty on the first moments. A critical point is a zero of all four partial derivatives; a topological saddle is a critical point that is neither a local minimum nor a local maximum.

Translating all angles and, for the centered kernel, shifting any single angle by \(\pi\), are exact isometries, so the teacher is reduced to directions \((0,\widehat\beta)\) with gap \(\widehat\beta\in[0,\pi)\) (centered) or \(\widehat\beta\in[0,\pi]\) (plain). A student is dead if its mass vanishes, the two students are colliding if \(\theta_0\equiv\theta_1\), separated otherwise. For separated students the two mass equations have the positive determinant \(\Delta_q=\kappa_q^2-\Phi_q(D)^2\), \(D=\theta_0-\theta_1\), and Cramer's rule gives \[ c_0=\frac{\kappa_qP_q(\theta_0)-\Phi_q(D)P_q(\theta_1)}{\Delta_q},\qquad c_1=\frac{\kappa_qP_q(\theta_1)-\Phi_q(D)P_q(\theta_0)}{\Delta_q}.\tag{M} \] The census of a teacher is the multiset of its minima, each tagged by family kind (fit / colliding / separated) and, for a spurious minimum, by whether both student masses are positive or of mixed sign. Saddles are not part of the census.

In both theorems the critical points are therefore always global minima, spurious local minima, or topological saddles — no local maxima and no degenerate types escape the three labels — and the two classifications intersect, on live students, exactly in the centered families whose first moments match, \(u=v\).

The census map

The map plots the gap \(\widehat\beta\) horizontally and the compressed mass ratio vertically — \(y=s_1/s_0\) folded so that \(y=\pm1\) is the seam \(\lvert s_0\rvert=\lvert s_1\rvert\) and \(y=0\) the one-teacher line — and colours a teacher by its census. The census can change only where a family is born, dies, or changes type, and for the colliding families those loci are mass-free: they are the zero sets of three Wronskian-type determinants in the gap and the direction alone.

Theorem 3(the centered census map, exactly)

The boundary set of the centered census consists of nine curves: the two branches of the torque lens (present for \(\beta\in[\beta^*_1,\beta^*_2]\), where \(\tan(\beta/2)=\pi/2-\beta/2\) and \(\beta^*_2=\pi-\beta^*_1\)), the two branches of the potential curve (spanning \(0<\beta<\pi\), with \(y\in(\gamma,1-\gamma)\), \(\gamma=\tfrac2\pi\arctan\tfrac2\pi\)), the lines \(y=0\) and \(y=\pm1\), and the columns \(\beta\in\{0,\tfrac\pi2,\pi\}\). On the map — a cylinder, \(y\) being a circle of circumference \(2\) — these cut exactly ten faces, and the census is constant on each:

Four distinct census values occur. Merging the five face pairs separated only by the measure-zero column \(\beta=\pi/2\) leaves exactly five regions of positive area, and these five regions carrying these four values are the entire centered map. In particular a centered teacher with masses of opposite sign never has a positive-mass spurious minimum, and no separated family is ever a minimum.

Theorem 4(plain-ReLU root counts, for every gap) and the plain map

For \(\beta\in(0,\pi)\) (the mirror \(\beta\mapsto2\pi-\beta\) is an exact symmetry) the potential determinant has no interior roots — the plain model has no analogue of the centered potential curve — and the torque determinant has interior roots only on the arc \(t\in(0,\beta)\): none for \(\beta<\beta^*_R\), a double root at the midpoint for \(\beta=\beta^*_R\), and one on each side of it for \(\beta>\beta^*_R\), where \(\beta^*_R=2u^*\) with \(\tan u^*=\pi-u^*\). Hence the colliding part of the plain census is exactly determined for every teacher.

Unlike the centered model, plain-ReLU separated families can be minima (Theorem 2), so the plain map has a second set of boundaries that is not mass-free. The drawn plain map is the finite-grid rendering of Theorem 2 at depth 8; its seven labelled faces are backed by interval-certified witnesses, by exhaustive branch-and-bound enumerations at 22 named teachers, and by Krawczyk-certified continuation on nine rectangles (30,278 certified steps) — but a global face arrangement for the plain map is not a theorem. The ledger COMPLETENESS.md states, claim by claim, what is proved, what is certified, and on which domain.

The Lean formalization

Two formal developments exist. The original proof grew with the research and spans several hundred modules; its public endpoints centered_two_student_full_classification and noncentered_two_student_full_classification are hypothesis-free: every configuration \((\beta,s,c,\theta)\) is covered. The repository behind this page, two-teacher-census, carries a short, self-contained re-proof on the vocabulary of the paper's Lean development: the same statements, a few files, no dependency on the research tree. Its status — which lemmas are proved and which are still open — is recorded in that repository's PROOF_PLAN.md and build certificate.

The explorer itself is theorem data: each table row is a constructor of the Lean classification, its angles and masses are evaluated from the row's closed forms (scalar roots are bracketed and bisected; they never decide criticality or type), and the map asset census-map.js is generated by driving the very same JavaScript through a headless shim, so the picture cannot drift from the table it claims to show.

Certificates

Where a computation rather than a proof script is the natural instrument — root enclosures, face witnesses, exhaustive enumerations — the repository ships interval-arithmetic certificates (200-bit enclosures, directed rounding) together with the scripts that regenerate and re-check them. The centered map is certified complete: 10 faces, 4 census values, 5 positive-area pieces, in exact agreement with the shipped map. The plain map's certified scope is listed in the completeness ledger.
