The two-teacher census
Intro Primer Explorer How to read About Paper

Explore every critical point of the Gaussian population loss landscape with a two hidden neuron student and teacher network.

Understanding loss landscapes is central to explaining neural-network training, yet their structure remains only partially understood even in simple models. In the project Learning Polytopes, which led to the paper Removing spurious minima for planar features by skip connections, we studied the smallest interesting case of teacher–student training: networks with two hidden neurons on planar Gaussian input. Even this case turned out to be surprisingly rich — but with agentic proof search we found closed formulas for all critical points for any teacher, and we mapped the sets of teacher parameters on which the types of local minima agree, the census. Below is an interactive presentation of that result. You can drag and drop the teacher parameters and study every critical point the resulting landscape admits.

Both networks read a planar Gaussian input \(x\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)\) through two hidden neurons. Teacher \((s,\beta)\) and student \((c,\theta)\) have the same shape: \[f_{s,\beta}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{1} s_i\,\sigma(\langle w_i,x\rangle)\;[\,+\langle b,x\rangle\,],\qquad w_i=(\cos\beta_i,\sin\beta_i).\] Plain network: \(\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}\), no skip. Skip network: the same plus a learned linear skip \(\langle b,x\rangle\); at the optimal \(b\) it computes the centered feature \(\sigma_C(z)=\tfrac12\lvert z\rvert\), so a direction becomes an unoriented line.

Generators. The generator of neuron \(i\) is the second-layer scalar times the first-layer vector, \(g_i=s_i\,w_i\): a vector of length \(\lvert s_i\rvert\) (the mass) in direction \(\beta_i\). Scaling symmetry. ReLU is positively homogeneous, \(\sigma(\lambda z)=\lambda\sigma(z)\) for \(\lambda>0\), so \((s_i,w_i)\mapsto(s_i/\lambda,\lambda w_i)\) leaves \(g_i\) and the network function unchanged. The loss therefore depends on each neuron only through its generator, and the critical points of the full parametrisation are exactly those of the (mass, direction) parametrisation drawn in the explorer.

The teacher is fixed; the student is scored by the population loss, the mean squared difference of the two network outputs over the Gaussian input: \[\mathscr L(c,\theta)=\tfrac12\,\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)}\bigl(f_{c,\theta}(x)-f_{s,\beta}(x)\bigr)^2 .\] Because both outputs are positively homogeneous in \(x\), the expectation depends only on the angles between generators, and Gaussian pair moments give the closed kernel form \(2\pi\mathscr L=\tfrac\kappa2(c_0^2+c_1^2)+c_0c_1\,\Phi(\theta_0-\theta_1)-\sum_i c_i\,P(\theta_i)+\mathrm{const}\), with \(\Phi_R(t)=(\pi-t)\cos t+\sin t\) for the plain and \(\Phi_C(t)=(\tfrac\pi2-t)\cos t+\sin t\) for the skip network on \([0,\pi]\), and the teacher potential \(P(t)=\sum_j s_j\,\Phi(t-\beta_j)\).

The student can copy the teacher, so the minimum value of \(\mathscr L\) is \(0\): the question is whether gradient descent can find it, i.e. which other minima the landscape has.

A student \((c,\theta)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\) is a critical point if \(\nabla\mathscr L(c,\theta)=0\). It is a local minimum if \(\mathscr L\ge\mathscr L(c,\theta)\) on a neighbourhood — strict if the inequality is strict away from the point, flat if a whole line of minima passes through it (here: both students on one direction, only the sum of their masses fixed). It is the global minimum if \(\mathscr L(c,\theta)=0\), the exact fit of the teacher; a spurious minimum if local but not global; a saddle if critical but neither a local minimum nor a local maximum.

Each spurious minimum gets a type: collided (co) if the two students share a direction, \(\theta_0\equiv\theta_1\), separated (sep) otherwise; with same-sign (+) or opposite-sign (±) masses \(c_0c_1\gtrless0\). The classification proves, for every teacher, the complete list of critical points and the type of each.

For a teacher \((s,\beta)\) write \(\mathsf M(s,\beta)\) for the multiset of types of its spurious minima, e.g. \(\{\mathrm{co}^{\pm}\}\) or \(\{2\times\mathrm{co}^{+},\mathrm{sep}^{\pm}\}\); the exact fit is present for every teacher and not listed. A census is a set of teachers with the same \(\mathsf M\).

Rotating, reflecting, swapping and jointly rescaling the teacher's generators does not change \(\mathsf M\), so a teacher is a point of a plane: horizontal the gap \(\widehat\beta=\beta_1-\beta_0\) (half a period suffices), vertical the mass ratio \(y\), folded so that \(y=\pm1\) is \(\lvert s_0\rvert=\lvert s_1\rvert\) and \(y=0\) a vanishing mass. The census map colours this plane by \(\mathsf M\). Censuses can also be curves or single points: teachers with equal masses, a zero mass, or a right angle, whose landscape is special. Click any region to load one of its teachers and see its critical points in the table.

How to read the map

  1. Choose the network type in the header. The skip network's census has five regions and only collided spurious minima; the plain network's has many more, including separated ones. The map's gap axis spans half a period because reflecting the gap leaves the census unchanged.
  2. Pick a teacher. Drag a blue generator in the left plot (its length is the mass, its direction the angle), use the sliders, or click a region of the map to load a teacher from it. Thick lines and rings are one-dimensional and zero-dimensional censuses: degenerate teachers (equal masses, a zero mass, right angles) whose landscape is special.
  3. Read the table. Every row is one family of critical points of the loaded teacher — the exact fit, collided pairs, separated pairs, dead students — with the angles and masses computed from the row's closed form and the type proved for that family. Click a row to draw its students in red. A row with "split free" is a whole line of critical points: only the sum of the masses is fixed, and the type may differ along the line.

About

This census is part of the project behind Removing spurious minima for planar features by skip connections (Zimmermann, Grillo, Balakin, Loho, 2026), where the two-neuron classification serves as the laboratory for the paper's general-width results; it is not itself part of the paper. Every row of the table is a family of critical points with its proved type, evaluated from closed forms; nothing on this page is a numerical Hessian. Page by Jakob Paul Zimmermann.
