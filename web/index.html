The two-teacher census
About Paper

Explore every critical point of the Gaussian population loss landscape with a two hidden neuron student and teacher network.

Understanding loss landscapes is central to explaining neural-network training, yet their structure remains only partially understood even in simple models. In the project Learning Polytopes, which led to the paper Removing spurious minima for planar features by skip connections, we started by studying the smallest interesting case of teacher–student training: networks with two hidden neurons on planar Gaussian input. Even this case turned out to be surprisingly rich — but with agentic proof search we found closed formulas for all critical points for any teacher, and we mapped the sets of teacher parameters on which the types of local minima agree, the census. Below is an interactive presentation of that result. You can drag and drop the teacher parameters and study every critical point the resulting landscape admits.

Both networks read a planar Gaussian input \(x\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)\) through two hidden neurons. Teacher \((s,\beta)\) and student \((c,\theta)\) have the same shape, \[f_{s,\beta}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{1} s_i\,\sigma(\langle w_i,x\rangle)\;[\,+\langle b,x\rangle\,],\qquad w_i=(\cos\beta_i,\sin\beta_i),\] with \(\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}\). The second-layer weight \(s_i\in\mathbb R\) is the mass of neuron \(i\) — positive or negative, both signs are admitted throughout — and the generator of the neuron is the second-layer scalar times the first-layer vector, \(g_i=s_i w_i\): a vector of length \(\lvert s_i\rvert\) in direction \(\beta_i\). Plain network: the formula without the bracket. Skip network: with the learned linear skip \(\langle b,x\rangle\); minimising over \(b\) first replaces each ReLU by its even part \(\sigma_C(z)=\mathrm{ReLU}(z)-\tfrac12 z=\tfrac12\lvert z\rvert\), so a neuron responds identically to \(x\) and \(-x\): its direction matters only up to sign, the critical points do not depend on that sign, and the explorer therefore draws a skip-network generator as a segment through the origin, centered at it.

Scaling symmetry. ReLU is positively homogeneous, \(\sigma(\lambda z)=\lambda\sigma(z)\) for \(\lambda>0\), so \((s_i,w_i)\mapsto(s_i/\lambda,\lambda w_i)\) changes neither the generator nor the network function, so the critical points of the full parametrisation are those of the (mass, direction) parametrisation used here. Population loss. The teacher is fixed and the student is scored by the mean squared difference of the outputs, \(\mathscr L(c,\theta)=\tfrac12\,\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)}\bigl(f_{c,\theta}(x)-f_{s,\beta}(x)\bigr)^2\).

A student \((c,\theta)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\) is a critical point if \(\nabla\mathscr L(c,\theta)=0\). A critical point is a local minimum if \(\mathscr L\ge\mathscr L(c,\theta)\) on a neighbourhood — strict if the inequality is strict away from the point, flat otherwise; a local maximum if \(\mathscr L\le\mathscr L(c,\theta)\) on a neighbourhood; a saddle if it is neither. The global minimum is a critical point at which \(\mathscr L\) attains its smallest value; in this setting it always exists and the value is \(0\), because the student can copy the teacher. A spurious minimum is a local minimum that is not global: this is what can trap gradient descent.

Each spurious minimum gets a type: collided (co) if the two students share a direction, \(\theta_0\equiv\theta_1\), separated (sep) otherwise; with same-sign (+) or opposite-sign (±) masses \(c_0c_1\gtrless0\). The classification gives, for every teacher, the complete list of critical points in closed form and the type of each; the table of the explorer lists them.

For a teacher \((s,\beta)\) write \(\mathsf M(s,\beta)\) for the multiset of types of its spurious minima, e.g. \(\{\mathrm{co}^{\pm}\}\) or \(\{2\times\mathrm{co}^{+},\mathrm{sep}^{\pm}\}\); the global minimum is present for every teacher and not listed. A census is a set of teachers with the same \(\mathsf M\).

Rotating, reflecting, swapping and jointly rescaling the teacher's generators does not change \(\mathsf M\), so a teacher is a point of a plane: horizontal the gap \(\widehat\beta=\beta_1-\beta_0\) (half a period suffices), vertical the mass ratio \(y\), folded so that \(y=\pm1\) is \(\lvert s_0\rvert=\lvert s_1\rvert\) and \(y=0\) a vanishing mass. The census map colours this plane by \(\mathsf M\). Censuses can also be curves or single points: teachers with equal masses, a zero mass, or a right angle, whose landscape is special. Click any region of the map to load one of its teachers and see its critical points in the table.

About

This census is part of the project behind Removing spurious minima for planar features by skip connections (Zimmermann, Grillo, Balakin, Loho, 2026), where the two-neuron classification serves as the laboratory for the paper's general-width results; it is not itself part of the paper.
